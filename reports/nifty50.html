The five rules · Nifty 50 · P/E ≤ 25 · EPS growth ≥ 12% · ROE ≥ 15% (financials 12%) · debt/equity ≤ 1.0 · ≥ 12% a year over a 5-year hold
  1. In the index. A member of the Nifty 50.
  2. Cheap. The P/E is 25 or less (the price less net cash, divided by the earnings per share the valuation uses).
  3. Growing. Earnings per share grew at least 12% a year over both the last 5 years and the last 3, with a profit in every one of those years.
  4. Quality. Return on equity at least 15% – banks, NBFCs and insurers at least 12%, and borrowings no more than 1.0 times equity (not for financials: lending is their business) – from the latest yearly statements; ROE is the year's net profit over the average equity.
  5. Worth holding. At least 12% a year expected over a 5-year hold, if growth slows to half its past rate (at most 15%) and the shares are sold at 20x earnings. Dividends are not counted.

Near miss = one rule short: a P/E up to 30 with growth met, growth down to 7% with the P/E met, or only the return short. Flags: promoter holding falling; pledged shares are not in the data yet.

Profit growth vs price growth

At a glance: A 15 · B 12 · C 12 · D 7 · n/m 4. Profits outran the price and cheap on value (A/B + green): AXISBANK, INFY, HDFCBANK. Price outran profits and expensive (D + red): BEL, DRREDDY, GRASIM.

How to read it. Growth is a yearly rate over about three years: for Indian companies Screener.in's compounded figures (net profit, latest three financial years; share price over three years); for the rest, Yahoo Finance statements from the financial year about three years back to the latest twelve months (diluted EPS, so buybacks and new shares count). P/E change = ((1 + price growth) ÷ (1 + profit growth)) ^ years − 1: negative means the share is cheaper relative to profits than at the start. Grade: A = P/E fell 25% or more · B = fell up to 25% · C = rose up to a third · D = rose more than a third · n/m = not comparable. Prices are in the exchange's currency; sales and profits as reported (m = millions, cr = crore). Profits include one-offs – check large jumps in the filing. A rising metal price lifts miners' profits faster than their prices (most gold miners grade A now); if the metal falls, profits fall faster too.
