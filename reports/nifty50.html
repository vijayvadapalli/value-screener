The five rules · Nifty 50 · P/E ≤ 15 · EPS growth ≥ 20% · promoters ≥ 60% · ≥ 12% a year over a 5-year hold
  1. In the index. A member of the Nifty 50 – not one of the stocks added by hand – switched off below.
  2. Cheap. The P/E is 15 or less (the price less net cash, divided by the earnings per share the valuation uses).
  3. Growing. Earnings per share grew at least 20% a year over both the last 5 years and the last 3, with a profit in every one of those years.
  4. Promoters. Promoters hold at least 60% of the shares – not applied here: the company files carry no promoter holding.
  5. Worth holding. At least 12% a year expected over a 5-year hold, if growth slows to half its past rate (at most 15%) and the shares are sold at 15x earnings. Dividends are not counted.

Near miss = one rule short: a P/E up to 20 with growth met, growth down to 15% with the P/E met, or only the return short. Flags: promoter holding falling; pledged shares are not in the data yet.

How the backtest was done

How it was done. On each date: today's members of the list (the index then is not in the data), the yearly EPS of the years whose results were out (60 days after March), the price that day, and the same rules as the screen above – P/E ≤ 15, EPS growth ≥ 20% over 5 and 3 years, every year profitable, ≥ 12% expected over the hold. The latest yearly EPS stands in for the last twelve months, there is no net cash, and cash conversion and bad loans are not checked (not in the data then). Returns to today: the picks equal-weighted with dividends reinvested; the index is its price only (Yahoo has no total-return index; dividends add about 1–1.5% a year). Demergers are not adjusted in the prices (e.g. Tata Motors).

Profit growth vs price growth

At a glance: A 16 · B 10 · C 15 · D 5 · n/m 4. Profits outran the price and cheap on value (A/B + green): AXISBANK, INFY, HDFCBANK. Price outran profits and expensive (D + red): DRREDDY, GRASIM.

How to read it. Growth is a yearly rate over about three years: for Indian companies Screener.in's compounded figures (net profit, latest three financial years; share price over three years); for the rest, Yahoo Finance statements from the financial year about three years back to the latest twelve months (diluted EPS, so buybacks and new shares count). P/E change = ((1 + price growth) ÷ (1 + profit growth)) ^ years − 1: negative means the share is cheaper relative to profits than at the start. Grade: A = P/E fell 25% or more · B = fell up to 25% · C = rose up to a third · D = rose more than a third · n/m = not comparable. Prices are in the exchange's currency; sales and profits as reported (m = millions, cr = crore). Profits include one-offs – check large jumps in the filing. A rising metal price lifts miners' profits faster than their prices (most gold miners grade A now); if the metal falls, profits fall faster too.
